01The Ice and the Promise
ILUM // 26 BBY — W // — MIN

The snow on Ilum doesn't just fall; it bites. Saelith pulled her robes tighter, the Echani-weave struggling against the gale. Around her, the gathering of younglings had thinned to silence — each one swallowed already by the cave, each one alone with whatever the ice would tell them about themselves.

She was thirteen years old and a long way from home. Home, of course, was a word the Order discouraged. She had been told this since she was four. She had also been told, more recently and more quietly, that she had a particular talent for not showing what she had been told.

— ⟡ —

The cave was warmer than the wind, which is the only kindness anyone could honestly say about it. The walls were ice but not the dead ice of the surface. They were lit from inside, from somewhere, by the particular blue light that the Echani called tilan — the colour, her grandmother had once said, of an honest answer.

She did not call her grandmother her grandmother. Not in front of anyone. The Order did not approve of grandmothers.

She walked.

— ⟡ —

Saelith reached for the crystal. It didn't hum. It screamed — in a frequency only a spy could understand. Not a sound. A shape: the shape of a question being answered before it was asked.

The Force does not always speak in voices. Sometimes it sets a single word on the table, and waits to see if you'll pick it up.

She picked it up.
