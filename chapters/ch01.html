CH.01The Ice and the Promise
PADAWAN // 26 BBY

The cold did not leave immediately. Even after the ramp had sealed and the transport's engines had begun their low, patient climb, Ilum stayed with you. It clung to boots, to breath, to the back of the throat. The kind of cold that made sound sharper and thoughts slower. The kind that took its time letting go.

Saelith sat near the bulkhead with her legs tucked in and her gloves still on. The crystal was in an inner pocket, close enough to her chest that she could feel the small weight of it shift when the ship shifted. Not warm. Not cold. Just there, in the way a held breath is there before you decide what to do with it.

Around her, the other Padawans had already begun to talk all at once.

Someone laughed too loudly at something that wasn't quite a joke. Someone else was holding their crystal up to the cabin lights, turning it, watching how the color caught — a deep blue that flickered between azure and cobalt as the ship's running lights pulsed. Mine's going to be green, the same Padawan was saying, with absolute certainty, I can already feel it. Saelith did not look up.

"Padawan Veyra."

Master Huyang's voice, dry, mechanical, very kind beneath the dryness. Saelith looked up.

"Your crystal, please. We are entering the construction phase shortly."

She unzipped the inner pocket of her cold-suit and set the crystal on the table between them. In the cabin lights it was — almost colorless. Pale. A faint internal frost, like a window in winter remembered rather than seen. When she tilted her head, a violet undercurrent moved through it, slow as something dreamed.

Huyang considered it for a long moment.

"Unusual," he said.

"Is it bad?"

"No. Merely unusual." A pause. The architect droid's optical receivers flickered through their patient cycle. "I have seen perhaps four crystals like this in eight centuries of service. Each one has been... particular. Particular crystals require particular sabers. Are you prepared to listen to it?"

Saelith looked at the crystal. The crystal, as far as she could tell, was not saying anything. But she did not say this, because she suspected Huyang would not consider that a useful answer, and also because she suspected that the not-saying-anything was, itself, a kind of saying.

"I think so," she said.

"Then we begin."

· · ·

The construction took her four hours. She had expected a kind of meditation. A long, slow process of attunement, alignments tested and refined. Instead, what she found was a problem. The crystal did not want a hilt that announced itself. It did not want decoration. It did not want the standard initiate's flush activator with the engraved Order sigil.

She rebuilt the housing three times. Each time, she made it plainer.

By the fourth attempt the hilt was a length of matte gray-black alloy with a flush activator and no markings of any kind. It looked less like a Jedi's lightsaber and more like the kind of tool a maintenance worker would clip to a belt and forget about. When she finally seated the crystal and closed the housing, the resonance settled into her hand the way breath settles into a sleeping child's chest. Yes, the crystal said, without saying anything. This.

· · ·

Master Calreth was waiting for her on Coruscant. She did not know him by sight, only by the brief description on the assignment slate — a Knight of moderate seniority, intelligence-trained, attached to no established lineage. When she came down the transport ramp into the Temple's receiving hall and a man in plain robes detached himself from one of the columns and walked toward her, she registered him the way she had been trained to register strangers: feet first, hands second, face last.

His feet were quiet on the polished stone. His hands were empty and visible, the way a person's hands are empty and visible when they have spent enough time being read by other people that they have stopped doing it accidentally.

His face, when she finally looked at it, was plain. Not in the dismissive sense — plain in the deliberate sense. The kind of face that had been chosen for not being remembered.

He stopped two paces from her, which was the correct distance.

"Padawan Veyra. I am Calreth. We have been assigned together. I trust the construction went well."

"Yes, Master."

"May I see?"

She handed him the saber. He took it, weighed it, ignited it for the briefest fraction of a second — the blade flashed pale blue-white with that violet undercurrent, thin and controlled and almost shy, and then was gone — and handed it back without comment.

"Good," he said.

Then, because he was watching her face and seemed to register that she was waiting for more: "It is a quiet saber. I requested a Padawan whose saber would not announce itself. There were three candidates. Master Yoda recommended you."

She kept pace as he turned and walked toward the Temple's deeper corridors.

"May I ask why, Master?"

"Why I requested such a Padawan, or why he recommended you?"

"Both."

A small sound that was almost — but not quite — a laugh.

"In time," said Master Calreth. "We have a long way to walk."

And they did.
