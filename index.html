01 // The Premise

Star Wars usually shows you the famous Jedi — the duels, the speeches, the dramatic falls. This is the other shelf.

Saelith Veyra is a Jedi the Council never quite knows what to do with: Echani, intelligence-trained, running cover identities since she was fourteen. She doesn't fight battles. She works rooms. She turns assets. She walks out of buildings she was never officially in, with information she's not allowed to admit she has.

"She didn't romanticize the Jedi tradition. She used what worked, and she paid for everything."

The novel begins on Ilum at thirteen and ends on Coruscant the night Anakin marches on the Temple. Everything in between is the slow education of a girl who was always going to see clearly, in a galaxy that was always going to fail to listen.

03 // Archive Maker

I've been a Star Wars fan since I was small enough that my world was mostly carpet. I've drawn the lightsabers, built the dioramas, argued about what's canon and what isn't. Saelith started as a single sentence I couldn't get out of my head: what if the most interesting Jedi in the room was the one nobody noticed?

So I'm writing her. Properly, slowly, for as long as it takes. Pre-Order-66 spy stories barely exist in canon and I want one — one that doesn't flinch from the moral compromise, one that takes the Echani and the intelligence work seriously, one that uses the visual language of comics where prose alone won't do.

This is also my long-term portfolio project. After the Empire arc, I'll start on original IP. The audience that grew up with Saelith is who I'll be writing for next.

— Datara
